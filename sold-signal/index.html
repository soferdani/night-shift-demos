Night 4/5 · what the loan amount will say about the price, and where it stops

Three nights ended on the same sentence: a confirmed sale, never a comp. That is still true. Nothing in the loan file is a price, and no join puts one there.

But the rule the loan had to satisfy is published. On a complete change of ownership the SBA requires a minimum 10% equity injection measured against total project cost. So the loan amount stops being only a loan amount:

loan ≤ (1 − 0.10) × project cost → project cost ≥ loan / 0.90

That is arithmetic on a published rule, not an estimate, and it needs nothing but the loan amount. Going from the project cost to the price needs assumptions — so this page keeps the two apart and never prints one in the other's clothes.

Three columns that are not about price

What the data decides is not the price. It is whether you may estimate one on this row at all. Not one of these three fields is about price.

fieldwhat it is forwhat it decides here
ApprovalDatewhen the loan was approved The 10% rule starts 1 June 2025. Before it, the standard in force since 2021 permitted zero-down structures — so on an older row the loan may be the whole project and the floor collapses.
TermInMonthsrepayment On a change of ownership the term is capped at 10 years unless commercial real estate is ≥51% of the purchase price, which allows 25. A 300-month row is mostly a building.
GrossApprovalthe loan At the $5M per-loan cap the loan stops tracking the deal. A capped loan says “at least this big” and nothing more, so the band is void, not wider.

The maturity column is a repayment field, and it is the only thing in the file that bounds how much of a purchase was property. Same shape as night 2's finding: the answer sits in a column nobody would read for it.

The rows it will price

Two kinds of number, never mixed. The floor is arithmetic on the rule. The band is an assumption set, stated so you can disagree with it.

The rows it refuses to price, and why

A blocked row is not a failed match. It is a row where printing a price would be printing the wrong thing — and the file says so by itself.

The one place the band can be falsified

Nothing public carries the sale price. A broker listing carries an asking price, so wherever leg A happens to work — a listing that named its legal entity — the band meets a number it did not produce.

Careful about the floor

The floor is on the project, not on the price. The price is one use inside the project, so a price below the floor is entirely possible. It just means the loan also funded something that was not the purchase.

What the file cannot settle, at any sample size

  • whether the change of ownership was complete or partial — the label does not say, and the injection rules for a partial change are not the same
  • how much of the loan was the purchase and how much was working capital
  • whether a seller note on full standby carried part of the injection (it may cover up to half of it)

So a priced row is a confirmed sale with a bounded deal size. Still not a comp: the price is estimated rather than observed, and there is no cash flow and no multiple anywhere on it.

Night 3: the one listing type that can’t hide what it is

A blind listing hides the legal entity on purpose. A franchise resale cannot: the brand is most of what is being sold, so it stays in the headline, and the loan row carries that brand in a field of its own. So the join runs brand to brand instead of name to name.
